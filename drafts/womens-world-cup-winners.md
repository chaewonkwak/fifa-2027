Five countries have won the FIFA Women's World Cup in its nine tournaments so far. The United States have won four times (1991, 1999, 2015 and 2019), Germany twice (2003 and 2007), and Norway (1995), Japan (2011) and Spain (2023) once each. Spain are the reigning champions: they beat England 1–0 in the 2023 final in Sydney. The tenth tournament will be played in Brazil in 2027.

## Patterns across nine tournaments

- **The United States have the most titles**, four, twice as many as any other country.
- **Germany won back-to-back titles**, in 2003 and 2007.
- **Spain became the fifth different champion** when they won in 2023.
- **The tournament has grown.** The 2023 edition, co-hosted by Australia and New Zealand, featured 32 teams, up from 24 at the previous tournament. Brazil 2027 keeps the 32-team format, with 64 matches.

For the players who scored the most goals across all these tournaments, see [Women's World Cup all-time top scorers](/womens-world-cup-top-scorers/).

## 2027: the tenth tournament, in Brazil

The 2027 Women's World Cup runs from 24 June to 25 July 2027 in eight Brazilian host cities, the first edition in South America. The final will be at the Maracanã in Rio de Janeiro, which will become only the third stadium to host both a men's and a women's World Cup final. The other two are the Råsunda Stadium in Solna, Sweden (men's final 1958, women's final 1995), which has since been demolished, and the Rose Bowl in Pasadena, United States (men's 1994, women's 1999).

See the [match schedule](/schedule/) for dates and venues, the [host cities map](/host-cities-map/) for where the matches are played, and [qualified teams](/teams/) for who is already through.

## How we checked these numbers

The 2023 and 2027 facts, and the 1995 and 1999 final venues, come from FIFA. For 1991 to 2019 we started from the sports data company Opta, cross-checked with ESPN and NBC Sports, and wrote a result only where Opta and at least one of the other two agreed. Six entries did not pass that test, either because the sources disagreed or because Opta did not cover them: the 1999, 2007 and 2019 runners-up and the 2003, 2011 and 2019 hosts. We checked all six against FIFA's own list of champions. One of them was a real disagreement: Opta names Canada as the 1999 runner-up, while FIFA, ESPN and NBC Sports all have China PR.
