São Paulo is Brazil's largest city and its business capital, a vast metropolis on a plateau inland from the coast. It is known for its food scene, museums and neighbourhoods that each feel like a city of their own.

## The stadium

Arena Itaquera, home of Corinthians and widely known by its sponsor name, Neo Química Arena, is in the far east zone of the city. It hosted the opening match of the 2014 men's World Cup. FIFA lists it as Arena Itaquera for this tournament.

## Matches in São Paulo

FIFA has confirmed that every host city stages at least seven matches, including at least one knockout match. Arena Itaquera is scheduled to host:

- Brazil's second group match, on Tuesday 29 June 2027
- Group-stage matches
- A quarter-final
- A semi-final
- The third-place match (the "bronze final")

Teams and kickoff times are not set yet. The final draw on 11 December 2026 will decide the groups.

## Getting to the stadium

The stadium is next to Corinthians-Itaquera station, on metro Line 3 (Red), which also connects with suburban trains. From central areas such as Sé or República, Line 3 runs straight there.

## Arriving in São Paulo

Most international flights use São Paulo/Guarulhos (GRU), north-east of the city. Congonhas (CGH), inside the city, is the main domestic airport. Viracopos (VCP), near Campinas, is a further option for some routes.

## Where to stay

Avenida Paulista, Jardins and Pinheiros are popular bases with plenty of hotels and restaurants. If getting to the stadium matters most, areas along Line 3 such as Tatuapé cut the journey time.

## Weather in June and July

São Paulo can feel surprisingly cool in June and July. Days are often mild and dry, but nights can be chilly, and grey, drizzly spells are common. Bring a warm layer for evening matches. See the [weather guide](/travel/weather-june-july/) to compare all eight host cities.
