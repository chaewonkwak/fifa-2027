Rio de Janeiro sits on Brazil's south-east coast, between granite peaks and long Atlantic beaches. It is the country's best-known city abroad and its football history runs deep, with the Maracanã at the centre of it.

## The stadium

The Maracanã is in the north zone of the city, a few kilometres inland from the beaches. It hosted the men's World Cup finals of 1950 and 2014, and will be one of only three stadiums to stage both a men's and a women's final.

## Matches in Rio de Janeiro

FIFA has confirmed that every host city stages at least seven matches, including at least one knockout match. The Maracanã is scheduled to host:

- The opening match, Brazil's first group game, on Thursday 24 June 2027
- Group-stage matches
- A quarter-final
- A semi-final
- The final, on Sunday 25 July 2027

Teams and kickoff times are not set yet. The final draw on 11 December 2026 will decide the groups.

## Getting to the stadium

Rio's metro has a Maracanã station right beside the stadium, which is usually the simplest way in on match days. From Copacabana, Ipanema or Botafogo the trip involves a transfer between lines.<!-- VERIFY: current metro lines serving Maracanã station and the transfer from the south zone -->

## Arriving in Rio de Janeiro

International flights land at Rio de Janeiro/Galeão (GIG), north of the centre. Santos Dumont (SDU), next to the city centre, handles most domestic flights, including the busy Rio–São Paulo route.

## Where to stay

Most visitors stay in the south zone: Copacabana and Ipanema for the beaches, or Botafogo and Flamengo for quicker metro access to the stadium.<!-- VERIFY: neighbourhood advice reviewed by a person who knows Rio -->

## Weather in June and July

June and July are winter in Rio, but the city stays mild: warm, often sunny days, cooler evenings and occasional rain. A light layer is enough for night matches. See the [weather guide](/travel/weather-june-july/) to compare all eight host cities.
